# [[690,234,3]] — balanced order-3 BB continuation on Z_15 x Z_23

## Direction & hypothesis

Continue the validated order-3 shared-factor branch near the schema limit on
a torus with both dimensions substantially larger than the original short
axis. Preserving factor order 3 was expected to retain roughly one-third rate
and the parent's witnessed `d <= 3` branch while probing whether the more
balanced `15x23` geometry survived deep self-refutation.

## What was searched

A structural transfer audit generated connected, exact-k continuations with
`l*m <= 350` and no board-fingerprint duplicate. Candidate 40003 was
included in the six-record balanced order-3 promotion batch. It uses a
`15x23` torus, 4+4 supports, check weight 8, exact `k=234`, and one Tanner
component of size 690. It was screened at 400 Python RIS trials and promoted
through 8k, 60k, and 1M accelerated rungs.

## Dead ends

No collapse occurred across the confirmation ladder, but the score gain is
rate-driven and the board FOM remains modest. Relative to the two
`[[696,236,3]]` continuations, this more balanced torus uses six fewer physical
qubits and encodes two fewer logical qubits; it is a distinct frontier tradeoff
rather than a strict improvement. Lower-order factor mutations projected
higher campaign scores but carried the same distance-collapse concern.

## Tools

Local structural transfer audit, deterministic periodic-BB campaign runner,
the repository BB/submission kit, `gf2_fast` with pair depth 16 and 8 threads,
trusted Python GF(2) witness checks, and the trusted local validator.

For the 60k and 1M rungs the runner reused the already persisted, schema-valid
8k Python candidate rather than repeating that expensive search. It required

recorded the source filename in provenance, saved each target under a new
non-overwriting path, and then persisted and Python-checked a fresh accelerated
witness. Thus reuse preserved the witness lineage without risking an in-memory
or print-only result. All artifacts remain local and unsubmitted.
